Miriam turned the brass key twice and the lighthouse door gave way. She had not climbed these stairs since the fire of 1908, when she was nine years old and small enough to hide beneath the lamp housing. Out past the headland the beam still swung its slow arc, the way it had every night for forty years. She reached for the railing and the salt-eaten iron come away in her hand.

Verification Checking
Consistency
“she was nine years old”

Your notes give Miriam’s birth as March 1902. The 1908 fire would make her six.

Logic
“the beam still swung its slow arc”

Chapter 4 decommissions the light in 1931. This scene is set in 1947, so the lamp is dark.

Grammar
“come away in her hand”

The chapter is in past tense. Use “came”.

What it checks

Three kinds of wrong, kept apart.

A misspelling and a broken timeline are not the same problem and do not deserve the same colour. Narwhal sorts by the nature of the error, not by where the contradicting fact happened to live.

Grammar

Agreement, tense, punctuation. Narwhal detects the language of the chapter first, so it corrects the language you are actually writing in, not the one your notes are in.

Consistency

A fact here that a fact elsewhere denies. Every other chapter and every note you keep (characters, world, bibliography) is read as context before this sentence is judged.

Logic

Events that cannot happen in the order you put them, or that break a rule your world has already set. The sentence is fine; what it describes is not.

Your files

Your book is a folder of Markdown files.

Chapters and notes are plain .md on your own disk. Open them in any editor, back them up like any other folder, and leave whenever you want. There is no database and no account holding your text.

Nothing is sent anywhere until you press Check text. The API key that talks to the model never ships inside the app.
